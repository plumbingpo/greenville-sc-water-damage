The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. Repair is a different job. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Greenville crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. They are two phases. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. Source control, so no new water enters. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Greenville

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • A musty odor has begun because materials stayed wet for a day.
  • The leak is fixed, yet cabinets, walls or flooring remain wet.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.
  • After a leak, the baseboards have swollen or started separating from the wall.

What a Professional Brings to Water Damage Mitigation

Mitigation is a series of judgment calls made quickly. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? It also gives the repair contractor a clear starting point, with no wet surprises behind new drywall.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Greenville Restoration Co:

1

Control the Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Extract the Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Set Up Containment

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A monthly look around toilets and inside sink cabinets catches drips, staining and spongy flooring.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Before heavy rain returns, caulk openings at doors, windows and roof penetrations.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
