Crawl Space Water Removal: What Greenville Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Damp air rises into the living space. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

What You Breathe Upstairs

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

How We Fix It

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.
